Free AI Tools for Video Creators: The Resources I Actually Use

No affiliate links, no fifty-logo roundup — the free AI tools and resources I actually use for scripting, voiceover, captions, stock footage and mockups, with free-tier limits noted.

Search “free AI tools for video” and you get the same fifty-logo roundup on every site, written by someone who has never cut a video in their life, stuffed with affiliate links to tools that stopped being free a year ago. This is the opposite of that: the short list of free tools and resources I actually have open while producing a SaaS product video, organized by the part of the job they help with. Nothing here is sponsored, and I am naming the free-tier limits, because that is the part those roundups always leave out.

Scripting & research

Before any tool opens, the job is turning a feature brief into a structure. A free-tier chat model (ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini's web app) handles the first pass well: paste the release notes, ask for five different angles on the opening line, and keep the one that does not sound like marketing copy. Perplexity's free tier is useful one step earlier, for checking how competitors talk about a similar feature before writing a word. Google's NotebookLM (free) is the one I reach for when the brief is messy — a jumble of Slack threads, a PRD and old scripts — because it can summarize across several documents at once instead of one at a time.

Needs your input: which of these you actually use day to day, and whether there is a tool here that is not it — swap in your real stack.

Voiceover & captions

For a rough-cut voiceover or a placeholder track while picking music, free text-to-speech has gotten good enough to ship on internal reviews. ElevenLabs' free tier gives a limited monthly character allowance, which is enough for a handful of 60–90 second scripts before it asks you to upgrade. For captions, CapCut's auto-caption feature is free and accurate enough for a first pass; YouTube Studio's built-in auto-captions are a free fallback if the video is already uploaded there. None of these replace a proofread, but they remove the part of captioning that used to be typing every word by hand.

Stock footage & music

This is the category that made the GetResponse post work, and it still holds: Pexels and Pixabay for free stock video and photos, no attribution required on most licenses; Mixkit for shorter, more modern B-roll clips that read better in a SaaS context than typical stock footage. For music, the YouTube Audio Library is free and commercially usable even outside YouTube, and Pixabay Music covers the gaps when I need something more specific in mood or length.

Mockups & UI assets

When the feature being advertised is not built yet, or the screenshots are not launch-ready, free mockup tools fill the gap. Figma Community has hundreds of free device-frame and UI-kit files that can be duplicated and edited directly. Screely turns a raw screenshot into a clean browser-frame image for free, in one click, which is often all a slide or thumbnail needs.
