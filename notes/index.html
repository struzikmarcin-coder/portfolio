AI video script

How to write an AI video script people watch to the end

“Most AI-written video scripts lose the viewer in the first five seconds. It is almost never the AI’s fault.”

Ask AI for a script and you will get the same thing every time: “Introducing the all-in-one solution that…” It reads like release notes with a voiceover, and viewers leave before the second sentence. The model is doing exactly what you asked. You asked it about the feature, not about the person watching.

The fix is a change of input, not a cleverer prompt. Start with the problem the viewer has on a Tuesday morning, make AI write five different first lines, and throw four away. The hook is the one part I still choose with my own gut.

AI video editing: 7 tasks to hand over today, and 3 you never should

“The best use of AI in editing is the work you never wanted to be good at.”

Start with the easy wins. Auto-captions, silence and filler removal, and resizing one video into vertical, square and wide are solved problems now. Doing them by hand in 2026 is a hobby, not a workflow. Those are three of the seven; the other four are in the full note.

Now the three I keep for myself: the pacing of a cut, the timing of UI animation against what is on screen, and the order of the story. AI gets all three roughly right, and “roughly right” is the exact distance between a video people finish and one they close.

AI tools for video

The AI tools for video I stopped using, and the 4 I would keep

“I tried a lot of them. Almost every one failed the same one-line test.”

Every few weeks a new tool promises to replace your video workflow. I try most of them once, on real work, because a demo hides exactly what makes a tool annoying after a week.

The test is one question: does this remove a step, or does it add one more thing I have to check? Tools that remove a step stay and become invisible. The rest leave a browser tab open for a month and then quietly close. A tool that has to be babysat is not saving time, it is just moving the work.
